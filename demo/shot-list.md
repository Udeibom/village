# The demo, beat by beat

The video is 1 minute 42 seconds. This is what is on screen at each moment,
so a voice-over can be recorded over it (or under it). The words are the
learner's to write; this is only the map of the picture.

| Time | On screen |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:03 | Title card: **Village** — "Lesson plans that fit the room you actually have." / "The AI writes the lesson. The code checks it." |
| 0:03–0:25 | The teacher fills in the request: grade, subject, topic, curriculum objective, class size, lesson length, and what the room allows ("No projector, no printing, no internet during the lesson") |
| 0:25–0:29 | The materials on hand: bottle tops 40, beans "a bag", measuring cups 2, chalk 1, blackboard 1 |
| 0:29–0:37 | Generate is pressed; the lesson arrives, and the panel opens beside it |

| 0:41–0:50 | The teacher clicks a dotted line in the lesson and types **marker pens**. The panel answers straight away: the lesson needs marker pens, which is not on your list. No model was asked — this is the code, in the browser |

## The two moments worth a voice

- **0:37–0:50.** The refusal, and then the disagreement. The model wrote the
  lesson; the code is the one that says no. Everything before this is setup.
- **0:54–1:06.** The lesson changing because the classroom changed. This is
  the same thing as a teacher asking for a second version — but it happens
  because the materials on the table are different.

## Recording the voice

- The video is silent on purpose; the on-screen text carries the story, so
  the voice can be an addition rather than a requirement.
- Under three minutes is the limit. There is room for roughly 150 words.
- Record it separately and lay it over the picture, or record it in one take
  while the video plays. Either is fine.
- Say who it is for and what problem it solves in the first ten seconds.
  Judges read the form and watch the video once.
